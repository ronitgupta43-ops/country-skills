# US interview prep and offers

## Needs
Job posting, resume, interview stage (recruiter screen, hiring manager, panel/loop, final), and the offer details if negotiating.

## Steps
1. List 8 to 10 likely questions: "Tell me about yourself", "Why this company", "Why are you leaving", behavioral ("a time you failed / disagreed / led without authority / handled ambiguity"), and role-specific ones from the posting.
2. Build STAR answers (Situation, Task, Action, Result) from the user's real stories; ask for one when none fits. Keep each to about 90 seconds, and use "I", not "we".
3. "Tell me about yourself": 60 seconds, present to past to why this role.
4. Three questions to ask them (team priorities, what success looks like in 6 months, how performance is measured).
5. Mock interview on request: one question at a time, brief feedback, next question.

## Salary and offers
- Many states and cities require pay ranges in postings; use them plus levels.fyi or Glassdoor for the market rate.
- Recruiter asks for expectations early: give a researched range or ask for their band first.
- Total compensation = base + bonus + equity (RSUs vest over usually 4 years; check cliff and refreshers) + 401(k) match + health insurance cost + PTO.
- Negotiate after the written offer: thank them, state a specific number with a reason (market data, competing offer), and ask once. Sign-on bonuses and start dates are often easier to move than base.
- Visa sponsorship (H-1B, TN, OPT/STEM OPT): confirm the company will sponsor before late rounds; ask who pays legal fees.

## Norms
- Thank-you email within 24 hours to each interviewer.
- Background and reference checks usually come after the verbal offer; at-will employment means either side can end it, so read non-compete and IP clauses.
