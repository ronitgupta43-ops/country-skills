# Tax for creators in the US (2026)

## Income
Brand deals, ad revenue (YouTube AdSense, TikTok), affiliate commissions, memberships/subscriptions, tips, merch, and products or trips received in exchange for content at fair market value. Report on Schedule C. All of it is taxable whether or not a 1099 arrives.

## 1099 forms in 2026
- 1099-NEC from brands/agencies only if they paid you $2,000+ in the year (was $600).
- 1099-K from payment platforms only above $20,000 and 200 transactions.
- No form doesn't mean no tax.
- Brands will ask for a W-9; consider an EIN instead of your SSN.

## Self-employment tax
15.3% (12.4% Social Security + 2.9% Medicare) on 92.35% of net self-employment earnings once net earnings reach $400; the Social Security part stops at the annual wage base. Half of it is deductible. Plus regular income tax: use us-income-tax for the bracket calculation.

## Quarterly estimated taxes
Required if you'll owe $1,000+ for the year. Due mid-April, June, September and January (Form 1040-ES). Safe harbor: pay 100% of last year's tax (110% if AGI was over $150,000) to avoid penalties. Rule of thumb: set aside 25 to 30% of every payment.

## Deductions (Schedule C; keep receipts)
Cameras, lights and mics (often fully deductible in year one), software and subscriptions, home office (exclusive use), phone/internet business share, travel for shoots, editors and managers, ads for your own channel, props used only for content. A SEP IRA or Solo 401(k) lets you save for retirement and lower taxable income.

## Other
- Gifted product you didn't ask for and don't promote may be treated differently; when in doubt, track its value.
- Paying US tax as a non-resident creator (e.g. Canadian or Indian): US brands withhold 30% on US-sourced income unless you file W-8BEN and a treaty applies.
- State income tax applies in most states.

End with: confirm with a CPA, especially before forming an LLC or S-corp.
