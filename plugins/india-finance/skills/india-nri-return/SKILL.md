# Moving back to India (returning NRI)

Ask first: which country they're leaving, expected return date, years spent abroad, and what they own there (bank, retirement accounts, stocks, property).

## 1. Residential status
- Resident if in India 182+ days in the tax year (other tests exist for people with high Indian income).
- Returning residents are often **RNOR** (resident but not ordinarily resident) for the first 2 to 3 years: they were non-resident in 9 of the previous 10 years, OR in India 729 days or less in the previous 7 years.
- While RNOR, income earned and received abroad is generally not taxed in India. Plan foreign sales/withdrawals for this window.
- After that they become ROR: worldwide income is taxable in India and foreign assets must be reported in Schedule FA of the return. Missing Schedule FA can mean heavy penalties under the black money law.

## 2. Bank accounts in India
- Tell banks about the change. NRE/NRO accounts must be redesignated as resident accounts.
- NRE balances can move to an RFC (Resident Foreign Currency) account to keep foreign currency.
- FCNR deposits can run to maturity.
- Update KYC and residential status with brokers and mutual funds (NRI demat → resident demat).

## 3. Accounts abroad
- Usually keep foreign bank and brokerage accounts open; they're allowed under LRS rules for returning residents.
- Retirement accounts (Canada RRSP/TFSA, US 401(k)/IRA, UK pension): tax treatment differs by country and the tax treaty (DTAA). Example: a Canadian TFSA is tax-free in Canada but India doesn't recognise it, so its income can become taxable once ROR. Suggest deciding before RNOR ends.
- Tell the foreign tax authority you've left (e.g. Canada: departure return, possible departure tax on investments).
- Claim foreign tax credit (Form 67) where tax is paid in both countries.

## 4. Other
- Aadhaar, PAN status update, health insurance (gap cover), driving licence, transfer of residence for baggage customs.

Always end with: rules depend on exact dates and amounts. Get a CA in India and a tax professional in the other country before moving large sums.
